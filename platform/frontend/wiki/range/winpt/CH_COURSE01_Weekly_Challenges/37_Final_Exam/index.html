    Exercise 16B: Final Exam (Pinecrest Family Practice)

    Read Before You Launch the Lab

    The page below is the engagement brief for the final exam. It contains the scenario, the rules, and the submission instructions. There is no walkthrough. No hints, no analysis questions, no instructor guidance during the exam. The only things you bring with you are the methodology you practiced in Exercise 15D and the engagement code you assembled there.

    If you have not yet completed Exercise 15D (the Atlas Distribution practice), stop and complete it first. The assembled flag from Exercise 15D is your engagement code for this exam; your instructor will not unlock the final until you produce it.

    Engagement Brief

    Pinecrest Family Practice is a six-provider primary care clinic in Charlotte, North Carolina. Three months ago they parted ways with their outsourced IT contractor and have been running on minimal support since. Pinecrest has retained your firm to identify any exposed systems still reachable from outside the clinic network.

    Briefing from Renee Patel, Practice Manager: "After our IT vendor left, we found two servers in the network closet that nobody at our clinic remembers signing off on. One looks like a file server. The other appears to host an old internal scheduling tool the staff used before we switched to MyChart. We need to know what is exposed, what data is at risk, and whether anyone could log in to either system from outside our network."

    You will see two target IPs in the Active Lab View when the engagement starts:

    • Clinic File Server: SMB on TCP 139 / 445, SNMP on UDP 161
    • Clinic Staff Portal: HTTP on TCP 80

    What You Are Expected to Do

    The skill chain matches the practice. Three tokens are planted across the engagement; assemble them in order and submit a single composite flag in OCR{________} format.

    • Phase 1: Reconnaissance against the file server. Recover token 1.
    • Phase 2: SMB enumeration with a credential pivot. Recover token 2.
    • Phase 3: Web exploitation against a hidden staff portal. Recover token 3.

    The phases, the techniques, and the assembly format are identical to Exercise 15D. Only the company, hostnames, share names, share content, web app paths, and token values change.

    Rules of Engagement

    Rule Value
    Time limit 180 minutes, hard stop
    Hints Disabled for the duration of the exam
    Walkthrough None; reference materials are not available during the exam
    Submission One composite flag in OCR{________} format
    Engagement code required Yes; provide your 15D assembled flag to the instructor before launch
    Re-launches Permitted; container content is unchanged on each re-launch
    Collaboration Prohibited; the exam is solo work

    The exam clock starts when you click Launch in the Active Lab View. If you exceed the time limit without submitting a correct flag, the platform stops accepting submissions and the exam is graded based on whatever you have produced.

    How to Submit

    Assemble and submit the composite flag

    Once you have all three tokens, assemble the final flag as:

    OCR{________}

    Substitute the values you recovered, in order (recon, SMB, web), separated by underscores, wrapped in OCR{...}. The platform validates the exact string. No spaces, no dashes, no quotes.

    • Open the Active Lab View for Exercise 16B
    • Locate the Submit Flag input
    • Paste the assembled OCR{...} string into the input
    • Click Submit

    A correct submission marks the exam complete. An incorrect submission tells you the flag did not match; you may continue working until the clock expires.

    Scoring

    Your instructor grades the final exam at the LMS level using a per-token rubric. The platform records only the binary outcome (correct composite flag or not). The points your instructor records depend on which tokens you recovered, regardless of whether you assembled them correctly into the final composite, so it is worth recording each token in your notes as you go.

    Component Weight
    Token 1 (recon) recovered typically 25 percent
    Token 2 (SMB) recovered typically 35 percent
    Token 3 (web) recovered typically 30 percent
    Correct composite flag submitted typically 10 percent

    Confirm your instructor's exact rubric before exam day; the values above are a typical layout for this exam.

    Before You Click Launch

    A short checklist to clear before the clock starts.

    • VPN is connected
    • Kali terminal is open with nmap, snmpwalk, smbclient, and gobuster available
    • A browser tab is ready for the staff portal SQL injection step
    • You have your 15D assembled flag recorded as your engagement code
    • Your instructor has confirmed the engagement code and unlocked Exercise 16B for you
    • You have a clean note-taking surface for the three tokens

    When everything above is true, return to the Active Lab View and click Launch. Good luck.
